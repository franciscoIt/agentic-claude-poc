- **Anchoring effects** — outputs shifting toward an initial number or framing given in the prompt
	- **The human version**: when people estimate a number, they get pulled toward whatever number was mentioned first — even if it's arbitrary. The classic experiment: ask people to spin a wheel that lands on either 10 or 65, then ask what percentage of UN countries are African. People who saw 65 guess higher than people who saw 10, even though the wheel was obviously random.

**In LLMs**: the model's output shifts toward a number, value, or framing introduced earlier in the prompt or conversation, even when that value is irrelevant or explicitly fabricated.

- **Framing effects** — different answers depending on how a question is worded, even if logically equivalent
	- **The human version**: logically identical information leads to different decisions depending on how it's worded. The classic case: a medical treatment described as "90% survival rate" gets chosen more often than the same treatment described as "10% mortality rate," even though they're the same fact.

**In LLMs**: the model's answer, tone, or recommendation shifts based on how a question is phrased, even when the underlying facts or logic are unchanged. 

- **Order effects** — earlier options in a list being favored
- **Sycophancy** — agreeing with the user's stated view rather than giving an independent answer
